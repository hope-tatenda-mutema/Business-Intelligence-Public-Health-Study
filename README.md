# Understanding Premature Mortality: A County-Level Analysis of YPLL

## Project Overview
[cite_start]This project investigates the structural drivers of **Years of Potential Life Lost (YPLL)** across 3,183 U.S. counties[cite: 109, 204]. [cite_start]Using the **2025 County Health Rankings & Roadmaps** dataset and **2024 Presidential Election results**, our team identified how socioeconomic disadvantage, geography, and political lean intersect to influence premature mortality[cite: 75, 83, 205].

## Key Findings
* [cite_start]**Poverty is the Primary Predictor**: Child poverty has a massive positive correlation ($r = 0.75$) with YPLL, carrying double the predictive weight of uninsured rates[cite: 114, 116].
* [cite_start]**Inequality Impact**: Income inequality (the gap between the 80th and 20th percentiles) shows a highly significant correlation ($r = 0.51$) with early death[cite: 120].
* [cite_start]**Regional Clustering**: Premature mortality is not random; it is heavily concentrated in the Deep South, Appalachia, and specific Western pockets[cite: 127].
* [cite_start]**Political Proxy**: While Strong GOP counties show higher median YPLL (~10,300) compared to Strong DEM counties (~8,400), this is largely explained by underlying socioeconomic disparities[cite: 121, 172, 173].

## Recommendations
1. [cite_start]**Place-Based Targeting**: Allocate funding to the top 10–20% highest-burden counties rather than uniform state policies[cite: 178, 223].
2. [cite_start]**Primary Care Investment**: Fund infrastructure in counties with fewer than 1 provider per 3,000 residents[cite: 186, 222].
3. [cite_start]**Medicaid Expansion**: Target states with uninsured rates >18% and high socioeconomic disadvantage[cite: 194, 221].
